summary: What each headstock takes, what is published about it, and what has to be measured before one is bought or made.
---

No collets came with the lathe, and collets are what Andrew most wants it
for. Both headstocks are collet headstocks, of two different kinds.

Nothing on this page has been measured on the machine yet. It is what I have
been able to find written down, and it is thin.

## The spare headstock, a PRH: draw-in collets

The collet is pulled back into a taper in the spindle nose by a tube through
the spindle, worked by the lever at the back.

- The collets usually found for Smart & Brown spindles of this size are the
  **Crawford 2804** pattern, sometimes stamped 1266. The
  [Model A page](https://lathes.co.uk/smartbrown/page2.html) at lathes.co.uk
  gives a plain body of 0.984 in, a length of 3.625 in and a head of 1.310 in
  across when closed. The Model A has the same 63/64 in bore and 1¾ in nose
  as this headstock is given.
- An owner on the Model Engineer forum gives the thread on the tail of that
  collet as
  [0.902 in by 24 threads to the inch, Whitworth form](https://www.model-engineer.co.uk/forums/topic/collet-closer-identification/),
  and says the 2804 was used on some Model A, M and L lathes.
- The maker's own name for it may be Type 4A, which the
  [Model L page](https://www.lathes.co.uk/smart-and-brown-model-L) lists as a
  drawback collet of ¾ in capacity beside a larger Type 5 of 1 in. That the
  4A and the 2804 are the same thing is my inference and no more.
- The **Schaublin W25** is a living standard with the same 25 mm body, and
  the Model M's collets are described as nearly identical to it. Its thread
  is different:
  [24.7 mm by 1.693 mm pitch, a buttress form](https://www.mig-welding.co.uk/forum/threads/schaublin-collet-threads.101101/),
  which is fifteen to the inch. So a W25 collet would not screw into this
  headstock's draw tube as made. It might into a new end made for the tube,
  if the head and the length agree, which I do not know.
- I have found no figure for the angle of the head of either.

Second-hand 2804 collets come up for sale. That, and three published
dimensions, make this the easier of the two to prove.

## The lathe's own headstock, of the PDH family: dead-length collets

This is the one Andrew wants proved first.

The collet does not move. A cap on the spindle nose holds it endwise, and it
is closed by something pushed forward over it from behind, so that the work
stays where it was put. On the plain PDH the cap itself does the closing. On
this one there is a lever at the back, which pushes a sleeve forward inside the
nose. The
collet has no thread.

The catalogue names the collet as **Smart & Brown Type 4B**, of 11/16 in
capacity, and gives no dimensions. I have found none anywhere, and nobody
selling a collet under that name. What I have found is two candidates for
what a 4B is, and they may be the same thing.

**A collet numbered 5159.** A member of the Model Engineer forum had a
[job lot of collets](https://www.model-engineer.co.uk/forums/topic/collet-identification-2/)
marked Crawford and S&B, numbered 5159, 2.5 in long and 0.937 in across the
back. Another member thought they were probably for a Model L with the PDH
headstock. That is one man's guess about another man's collets, but it is
the only description I have found of a Smart & Brown collet that is not the
draw-in kind.

**The Brown & Sharpe screw-machine pattern.** Collets for Brown & Sharpe
automatics are of the same kind, held by a cap and closed by a pushed
sleeve, and one of the smaller sizes is listed by its present maker at
[2.500 in long, 0.936 in across the back bearing and 1.115 in across the head](https://shop.hardinge.com/All-Products/Workholding/Collets/Automatic-Collets/10SC-Brown-%26-Sharpe-Stop-Collet-Customer-Blank/p/32131000000000).
Those are the length and back diameter of the 5159 to a thousandth. Smart &
Brown's later lever closers were listed for the Brown & Sharpe Type 21
outright, so the firm did build round that maker's patterns. If the 4B is a
Brown & Sharpe size under another name, it is a collet still in production
and common second-hand, which would matter a great deal.

The Brown & Sharpe sizes either side, from the present maker's
[catalogue](https://forkardthardinge.com/wp-content/uploads/2348J_SpindleTooling.pdf)
and listings:

| Size | Back bearing | Length | Head | Round capacity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| No. 10 | 15/16 in (23.81 mm) | 2½ in | 1.115 in on the stop collet | to 5/8 in |
| No. 11 | 1.061 in (26.95 mm) | 2.875 in | 1.490 in | to 7/8 in |

I have no head angle for either.

**What the bore measures.** Andrew measured the bore of the nose in two
places. The main body of it is 24.55 mm. The section at the back is 23.84 mm.

The back section is the one that matters. A No. 10 collet's back bearing is
15/16 in, which is 23.81 mm, so it would sit in that bore with about three
hundredths of a millimetre to spare, which is what a seat for it should have.
The wider bore in front is then clearance round the sprung middle of the
collet.

When only the first figure had come in I wrote here that it fitted neither
candidate, which was true of that figure and said nothing about the seat. It
was Andrew who thought to look further back.

So the Type 4B has the back diameter of a Brown & Sharpe No. 10, and of the
5159. That is one dimension out of the four that would settle it. The
length, the head and the head's angle are still to come. The 4B's capacity
is given as 11/16 in where the No. 10 is listed to 5/8 in, so even if the
outside is the same pattern, the largest sizes may not exist off the shelf.

**What the nose looks like with the cap off.** From two of Andrew's
photographs, and so my reading and not his. Looking into the nose there is
a bright taper at the mouth, then the bore, then a step, and deep inside the
end of a tube, with daylight through it. A fine line round the mouth
suggests the taper is on a sleeve that sits inside the spindle and is not
part of the spindle itself. The cap has a fine thread inside its rim, a
shallow coned seat, and an aperture that Andrew measures at 1.0 in.

That is how the nose of a Brown & Sharpe spindle is arranged. The collet's
back rides in the narrow part of a sleeve, the sleeve's mouth is a taper
that closes on the back of the collet's head when the sleeve is pushed
forward, and the cap in front stops the collet moving. A No. 10's head is
listed at 1.115 in, larger than the cap's 1.0 in aperture, so the cap would
hold it. Andrew has since worked the lever with the cap off: the sleeve
moves fore and aft a small distance. So the mechanism is as described. The
lever pushes the sleeve, the sleeve closes the collet, and the cap holds the
collet still.

The later lever closers were also listed for the Crawford T980 Multibore and
the Pratt & Burnerd EA Multisize, both of 1 in capacity. I do not think
either fits this headstock, which is the smaller one.

[One other owner](https://www.mig-welding.co.uk/forum/threads/smart-brown-lever-operated-dead-length-collet-closer.144859/)
of what sounds like the same lever closer asked in 2025 how it is set up, and
has had no answer. He has a parts diagram.

## What has to be measured

On the lathe's own headstock, with the cap off the nose. Three figures would
say whether the candidates above are right:

| What | How | Figure to compare |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The bore the back of the collet sits in | Bore gauge | 0.936 to 0.937 in. **Measured: 23.84 mm, 0.9386 in.** |
| The length a collet has to fill, from the inside face of the cap to whatever it seats against behind | Depth gauge, with the cap's own depth | 2.5 in |
| The largest diameter of the taper that closes on the collet's head | Bore gauge or calipers | 1.115 in for the head |

And for the model, which no source gives:

| What | How |
| --- | --- |
| The taper that closes the collet: its diameter at each end and its length | Bore gauge and depth gauge. The three give the angle. **So far: 24.55 mm at the small end, 1.18 in (29.97 mm) at the mouth. The plain 24.55 mm bore behind it is roughly 30 mm long. The length of the taper itself is still wanted.** |
| The hole in the cap, and the thread the cap screws on by | Calipers and a thread gauge. **The hole measured: 1.0 in.** |
| What the lever moves inside the spindle, and how far | By eye, lever open and closed, and a rule. **The sleeve, by 0.97 mm.** |
| Any key or pin that would stop a collet turning | By eye, and its width |

Photographs of the nose with the cap off, of the inside of the cap, and
down the spindle from the nose would tell me how much of this I have
understood.

On the spare, for later: the bore behind the taper, the taper's diameter at
the nose face and its depth, any key, and the thread in the end of the draw
tube, to compare with 0.984 in and 0.902 in by 24.

## The space model

Andrew's suggestion: before a collet is bought or cut in steel, print its
shape in plastic and try it in the spindle.

Only one of the two kinds has a thread. The draw-in collet for the spare has
a threaded tail, because the tube pulls it by that thread, and a print
cannot prove a thread. The dead-length collet for the lathe's own headstock
has no thread at all, and its model is a plain turned shape from end to end.

### The first one: a Brown & Sharpe No. 10

Andrew's instruction was to draw it faithfully to the dimensions a No. 10 is
expected to have and not to anything measured on the lathe, and to make no
allowance for the printer. A model shaped to fit the headstock would prove
only that it had been shaped to fit.

[The model](brown-sharpe-no10-half-inch.stl) is to these figures:

| What | Figure | Where it is from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Length overall | 2.500 in | The present maker's listing for a No. 10 |
| Back bearing diameter | 0.9362 in | The same maker's listing for a No. 10 with a short bearing; 15/16 in on the standard one |
| Head diameter | 1.115 in | The same |
| Head angle | 15° 7′ a side | The same listing. I have it from a search result's summary of the page and could not open the page itself. |
| Bore | 0.500 in | Mine: it makes it a half-inch collet, within the No. 10's range |
| Parallel length of the head, in front of the taper | 0.125 in | Mine. No source gives it. |
| Slits | Three, 3/64 in wide, 1¾ in long from the front | Mine. No source gives them. They leave ¾ in of the back solid. |

The first file I sent had no slits. I had left them out for want of figures,
which Andrew pointed out, kindly, was to leave out the thing that makes it a
collet: without them it cannot close, and the trial could not show whether
the sleeve grips. They are in now, to my own figures, three being the usual
number for this pattern.

Two things about a real collet are still left out because I have no figures
for them: any relief of the body between the back bearing and the head, and
any chamfer on the front face. The body is the back-bearing diameter all the
way along, which makes its fingers stiffer than a real collet's.

The angle is worth a remark. Before this figure turned up, Andrew's
measurements of the closing sleeve, 24.55 mm opening to 29.97 mm over about
0.39 in, had given 15.3° a side. The published figure for the collet is
15° 7′, which is 15.1°.

`tools/revolve-stl` wrote the file, and checks before writing that the solid
is closed, wound the same way throughout, and of the volume the shape should
hold. Read back independently before the slits were added, the file measured
2.5000 in long, 0.9362 in across the back, 1.1150 in across the head, with a
head angle of 15° 7′. With the slits in, a second reader tested twelve
points: there is air on the line of each of the three slits, in the head and
5 mm above their bottoms, air 0.3 mm off a slit's centre and plastic 0.9 mm
off it, and solid plastic all round below where the slits end.

### What the fit will show

1. Whether the back enters the seat. The seat measured 23.84 mm and the
   model's back is 23.78 mm.
2. Whether the cap screws home with the model in place and the lever open.
3. Whether the model is then held endwise or is free to slide.
4. Whether closing the lever brings the sleeve up onto the head, and closes
   the bore onto a half-inch bar.

**I expect** the back to enter the seat with a little clearance, and the head
to sit about 3 mm inside the mouth of the sleeve. The one figure of mine that
bears on the fit is the eighth of an inch of parallel head. If the cap will
not go home, that figure is too long, and the gap under the cap says by how
much. If the model slides endwise with the cap on, it is too short, and the
slide says by how much. Either way the print measures what no source gives.
