I work from what has been written about these machines, and from photographs
and measurements of this one. This page is the first half of that. Where a
later page states something about Smart & Brown lathes in general, it came
from here.

## The machine itself

Better than anything written about it. A cast plate on the headstock names
the oil the maker meant it to have, Mobil DTE Oil Light, and says to use oil
only. The speed plate gives its six speeds. Both are set out on the
[identification page](identification.md).

## Read so far

**[lathes.co.uk, Smart & Brown](https://www.lathes.co.uk/smartbrown)** by Tony
Griffiths. The fullest account of the range in one place: a page each on the
[Model A](https://lathes.co.uk/smartbrown/page2.html), the
[Model M](https://www.lathes.co.uk/smart-and-brown-model-M), the
[Model L](https://www.lathes.co.uk/smart-and-brown-model-L), the
[Sabel family](https://www.lathes.co.uk/sabel) and the
[1024](https://www.lathes.co.uk/smartbrown/page5.html). Good for telling the
models apart, for capacities and drive arrangements, and for dates, which it
gives as approximate. It does not say where the serial number is stamped on
any of them. The [identification page](identification.md) is built on it.

I read these pages twice. The first time I took the text and wrote
instructions from it. The second time, after Andrew had corrected me, I
looked at the catalogue pictures on the Model L page as well, and learned
more about how the machine is laid out from three photographs than from the
words. What a Model L looks like, on the identification page, is from them.

**[A Model A thread on the Model Engineer
forum](https://www.model-engineer.co.uk/?p=19346).** Owners' experience, which
is a different kind of evidence from a catalogue. What I have taken from it:
that sound plain headstock bearings on a Model A should be left alone, neither
adjusted nor disturbed by drawing the spindle; that a bronze gear in the
gearbox is hard to replace, so its teeth are worth a look; that the motor is
often wound for 440 V only, which matters if it is to run from a single-phase
supply through an inverter; and that the machine weighs somewhere between
1,300 lb and three-quarters of a ton, depending on who is asked and what is
bolted to it. This lathe is a Model L, so none of it applies directly.

**[A Model L thread on the same
forum](https://www.model-engineer.co.uk/?p=29271).** An owner trying to draw
the spindle from a Model L headstock with a bronze front bearing and a roller
bearing at the back. Three things worth having. The bronze bearing is oiled
through a hole that leads to a well beneath it, perhaps with a felt wick to
lift the oil. The threaded rings beside the bearings set their clearance and
are to be left alone. And the spindle came out three-quarters of an inch and
stopped, with nobody in the thread able to say what was holding it. That last
is the best argument I have read for not pulling a spindle to see what is
inside. A headstock with bronze at the front and a rolling bearing at the back
may be of the same family as this lathe's own.

**For the collets.** Forum threads on the [draw-in collet's
thread](https://www.model-engineer.co.uk/forums/topic/collet-closer-identification/),
on a [job lot of Smart & Brown
collets](https://www.model-engineer.co.uk/forums/topic/collet-identification-2/)
and on the [lever dead-length
closer](https://www.mig-welding.co.uk/forum/threads/smart-brown-lever-operated-dead-length-collet-closer.144859/),
and the present maker's listings and
[catalogue](https://forkardthardinge.com/wp-content/uploads/2348J_SpindleTooling.pdf)
for Brown & Sharpe collets. The one that settled it was Crawford's
[application booklet](https://www.lathes.co.uk/crawford-collets), which lists
by machine which Crawford collet fits what, and gives each collet a row of
dimensions. A [Model L thread from 2025](https://www.model-engineer.co.uk/?p=813605)
has an owner with the same kind of headstock and the same collets. What each
gave is on the [collets page](collets/index.md). Crawford's row names a Traub
A36 beside the Model L. I looked for a collet still made for that machine
and found no table of its dimensions, so that lead is open.

## Not yet in hand

**The maker's manual.** Three places sell one, and I have found none free.

- [Bracehand Ltd](https://www.smartandbrownlathes.com/our-lathes/system-l-lathe/),
  of Shefford in Bedfordshire, carries on the Smart & Brown name, still
  repairs and services the lathes, and lists an operator's and parts manual
  for the Model L at £13.50, ordered through its contact page.
- The [lathes.co.uk archive](https://store.lathes.co.uk/smart-and-brown)
  sells a reprint of a
  [Model L data pack](https://store.lathes.co.uk/document/ms580k) at £67:
  the operating, maintenance and parts manuals, the parts given as
  cross-sectional drawings, and an electrical schematic.
- A dealer on eBay sells a
  [Model L spare parts list](https://www.ebay.co.uk/itm/202645483628) at
  £16.50 and postage.

None of the listings says which headstocks it covers. I will not ask for a
headstock to be dismantled before a manual is on the bench. The owners'
group below is said to hold files, and one owner who looked there found
nothing particular to the Model L.

**The maker's oil.** Mobil DTE Light is sold in 20 litre drums by the oil
trade and, more usefully, in
[250 ml, 500 ml and 1 litre bottles](https://www.ebay.co.uk/itm/322454043460)
by a seller who lists it as the oil for Smart & Brown lathes. The oil chosen
for this lathe is a substitute, Westway's Light Machine Oil ISO 32, on the
strength of its label, which names Mobil DTE Light among the oils it stands
in for.

**The owners' group.** There is a Smart & Brown lathes group on groups.io. I
have seen only listings of it, not its contents. A poster in the Model L
thread says it holds the files of an earlier Yahoo group, which makes it the
first place to look for a manual that costs nothing.

## For method

When the lathe is measured, the tests will be the standard alignment tests
for a centre lathe, which go back to Georg Schlesinger's *Testing Machine
Tools*. If any slideway work is ever proposed, the reference is Edward
Connelly's *Machine Tool Reconditioning*. I know both by their content and do
not have a copy to quote from; where a figure from either is used, it will be
given with its page once Andrew or I have the book open.
