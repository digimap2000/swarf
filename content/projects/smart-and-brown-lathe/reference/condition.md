photographs and from what he has told me, and each item says which. He has
turned the spindle and wound the slides by hand and finds them all smooth,
and nothing he has seen looks terrible to him.

## Open

### Oilways, both headstocks

Total-loss. Each bearing has a pocket above it and nothing below: the
underside of the headstock is an open frame, and oil that has passed a
bearing drops out inside the casting. On the spare, oil put in at the top
runs straight through. On the fitted one, the inside of the casting is coated
with black oily dirt, heaviest under the front bearing, and filled pockets do
not fall that Andrew can see while the spindle is turned by hand. A sound
close bearing and a blocked oilway would both do that. *Known from Andrew and
his photograph of the underside.*

### Grease at the back bearing

The pocket under the nipple on the lathe's own headstock was packed with
grease, against the maker's plate, which says oil only. Andrew cleaned it
out. The back bearing is a pair of ball races, which grease does not harm the
way it harms bronze, so this is less urgent than I first made it. Find out
from the manual what the maker meant it to have, and measure its lift and end
float, which should both be nothing. *Andrew, his photograph down the hole,
and the catalogue's account of the PDH.*

### Oil-hole plugs

A plain hole above each spindle bearing, four in all, under the pulley cover.
The catalogue cutaway shows each closed by a small knurled plug with a short
stem. All four are gone. One hole had a nipple pushed into it, which Andrew
has taken out. Keep the holes covered, and make four plugs once the holes
are measured. *Andrew, who looked at all four, and the cutaway he found.*

### Bearing adjusting rings

The notched ring behind each nose is bruised beside its notches, as if driven
round with a punch. The cutaway shows these rings set the bronze bearings.
Leave them alone, learn from the spindle play what the punching did, and make
a C-spanner to fit before either is moved. *Photographs of both noses, and
the cutaway.*

### Spindle oil

The maker's plate asks for Mobil DTE Oil Light, an ISO VG 32. To hand are
Shell Morlina S2 BL 10 (VG 10) and Shell Tellus 68 (VG 68). The thin one is
for turning by hand and measuring, the Tellus for the slideways. For the
spindle under power, an ISO VG 32 bought for it. Two parts Tellus to one
Morlina would come to about the right viscosity, but one has a zinc additive
and the other is made without, and I have found nothing to say they sit well
together. *Andrew, and the oils' data sheets.*

### Bed

It came with the headstock, slide rest and tailstock resting on it, not
fixed. Andrew lifted them off and cleaned it with T-Cut and a Brillo pad. A
groove is cut across its front bevel near the headstock end, which looks
deliberate to both of us. About 32¾ in long, by Andrew's tape as I read it
from his photograph. To measure for wear, and oil against rust. *Andrew, and
his photographs.*

### Collets

None came with it, and they are what Andrew most wants the lathe for. The
lathe's own headstock takes dead-length collets, and its seat measures as a
fit for a Brown & Sharpe No. 10. The spare takes draw-in collets of a
published pattern. Details and what is still to measure are on the
[collets page](collets/index.md). *Andrew's measurements, and published
figures.*

### Belt

There is none. A flat belt has to be found or made. The catalogue gives one
inch wide, to be checked against the pulleys. *Andrew.*

### Motor

Out of the stand. A single-speed Brook, 2 h.p., 400/440 V three-phase, where
the lathe's change-pole switch and speed plate are for a motor with two
speeds. It was loose when Andrew got the lathe. There is a platform for it in
the stand with a lever to slacken the belt, and two stepped pulleys for it.
Andrew's inverter gives up to 415 V three-phase, which suits it. Whether the
two switches can be kept as signals to the inverter is settled at the
electrical stage. *Its plate, and the plates on the switches.*

### Coolant

A pipe, and a tray made as a basin with a brass drain plug. Pump and tank not
yet looked for. Andrew wants it working in the end. *Andrew.*

### Chuck backplate

A three-jaw chuck came with the lathe, and a backplate for it threaded for
the nose of the spare headstock, the only one of the two that can carry it,
and otherwise unmachined. Machine it on the spare's own spindle
once the lathe runs on that headstock, so that it runs true to it. *Andrew.*

### Spare headstock, a PRH

Complete but for its closer lever. Oil put in its pockets runs straight
through. Its bearings have not been measured. It is the only headstock here
that can hold work, so it is not parked. *Photograph, and Andrew.*

## Good

### Front spindle bearing

On the lathe's own headstock, clamped to the bed: no lift that a 0.01 mm
indicator can show. Next, the same reading at the back, and the end float.
*Andrew, measuring on the collet-closing cap, left a little proud of the
nose.*

## Parked

### Spare tailstock

The one with set-over. Loose in the tray, most of its paint gone, held
together with a cable tie. Neither of us sees a need for it yet. Oil it
against rust and leave it. *Photograph, and Andrew.*

## Noted, for the finishing stage

### Pulley cover

Off the headstock and lying in the tray. Andrew has the covers for both
headstocks. Refit after the headstock has been surveyed.

### Paint

A grey-blue, flaking, over a turquoise blue that shows at the chips, round
the plates and across most of one stand door, so it has been repainted at
least once. Before stripping, lift a plate to find the colour underneath.
*Photographs of the plates and the headstock.*

### Bright steel on the closer

The drum and the lever are peppered with rust. *Photograph of the left-hand
end.*
