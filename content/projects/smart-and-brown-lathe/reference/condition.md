---
title: Condition register
summary: Every fault found so far, what shows it, what is to be done about it, and where that has got to.
---

Nothing has been measured yet. Everything here so far comes from Andrew's
photographs and from what he has told me, and says so. He has turned the
spindle and wound the slides by hand and finds them all smooth, and nothing
he has seen looks terrible to him.

| What | Found | How it is known | Decision | State |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Oil-hole plugs, both headstocks | There is a plain hole above each spindle bearing, four in all. The catalogue cutaway shows each closed by a small knurled plug. All four plugs are gone. One hole, at the back of the fitted headstock, had a nipple pushed into it, which Andrew has taken out. | Andrew, who looked at all four, and the cutaway he found. | Keep the holes covered. Make four plugs once the holes have been measured. | Open |
| Grease in the back bearing's oilway, fitted headstock | The pocket under the nipple was packed with grease, on a bearing the maker's plate says is to have oil only. Andrew cleaned the pocket out. Both pockets on this headstock then held oil to the brim. How far the grease has gone into the bearing is not known. | Andrew, and his photograph down the hole. | Do not run the lathe until oil is shown to pass through this bearing. Work thin oil through it by turning the spindle by hand, and watch what comes out at the ends. | Open |
| Oilways, spare headstock | Oil put in at the top runs straight through and out of the underside. | Andrew. | Find where it comes out. The spare is the easiest way to learn how the oilways of both are made. | Open |
| Bearing adjusting rings, both headstocks | The notched ring behind each nose is bruised beside its notches, as if driven round with a punch. The catalogue cutaway shows these rings to be what sets the bronze bearings. | Photographs of both noses, and the cutaway. | Leave them alone. Measure the play in the spindle to learn what the punching did. Make a C-spanner to fit before either is ever moved. | Open |
| Spindle oil | The maker's plate asks for Mobil DTE Oil Light, which is ISO VG 32. The oils to hand are Shell Morlina S2 BL 10, a spindle oil of ISO VG 10, and Shell Tellus 68, a hydraulic oil of ISO VG 68: one about a third of the viscosity wanted and the other about twice it. | Andrew, and the oils' data sheets. | The thin oil for turning by hand and measuring. The Tellus for the slideways, which it suits. For the spindle under power, an ISO VG 32 oil. Two parts of the Tellus to one of the Morlina would come to about that viscosity by the usual blending rule, but one has a zinc additive and the other is made without, and I have found nothing that says they sit well together; I would sooner Andrew bought the right grade. | Open |
| Collets | None came with it. The headstock has a lever collet closer and nothing to close. | Andrew. | Nothing until the spindle's collet seat has been measured. Then decide between finding the maker's collets and making something that takes a common one. The chuck will do for now. | Open |
| Chuck backplate | Threaded for the nose but otherwise unmachined. | Andrew. | Machine it on this lathe's own spindle once the lathe runs, so that it is true to this spindle. A good first job for it. | Open |
| Spare tailstock, with set-over | Loose in the tray, most of its paint gone, held together with a cable tie. | Photograph, and Andrew. | Park it. Andrew sees no need for it yet and neither do I. Oil it against rust and leave it. | Parked |
| Spare headstock | Complete but for its closer lever. | Photograph. | Park it. Keep it oiled and its oil holes covered. | Parked |
| Belt | There is none. | Andrew. | A flat belt has to be found or made before it can run. The catalogue gives one inch wide; to be checked against the pulleys. | Open |
| Motor | Out of the stand. A single-speed Brook, 2 h.p., 400/440 V three-phase, where the lathe's change-pole switch and speed plate are for a motor with two speeds. There is a platform for it in the stand, with a lever to slacken the belt. | Its plate, in a photograph, and the plates on the switches. | Andrew's inverter gives up to 415 V three-phase, which suits it. Settle at the electrical stage whether the two switches can be kept in use as signals to the inverter. It was loose when Andrew got the lathe, and there are two stepped pulleys for it. | Open |
| Pulley cover | Off the headstock and lying in the tray. Andrew has the covers for both headstocks. | Andrew, and the photograph of the whole machine. | Refit after the headstock has been surveyed. | Noted |
| Coolant | A pipe, and a tray made as a basin with a brass drain plug. Pump and tank not yet looked for. | Andrew. | He wants it working in the end. Find out what survives when the stand is surveyed. | Open |
| Paint | A grey-blue, flaking, over a turquoise blue that shows at the chips, round the plates and across most of one stand door. Repainted at least once. | Photographs of the plates and the headstock. | Strip and repaint at the finishing stage. Before that, lift a plate to find the colour underneath it. | Noted |
| Bright steel on the closer | The drum and the lever are peppered with rust. | Photograph of the left-hand end. | Finishing stage. | Noted |
