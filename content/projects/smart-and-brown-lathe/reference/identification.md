---
title: Telling the models apart
summary: The lathes Smart & Brown made and what distinguishes each, as a guide to naming this one.
---

"A Smart & Brown lathe" does not say much. The firm, at Biggleswade in
Bedfordshire, built lathes from the early 1940s into the 1990s, and they have
little in common beyond the name and the class of work they were meant for.
Which one this is decides how its spindle runs, how it is oiled, what fits its
nose and which manual applies.

Everything in the table is from Tony Griffiths' pages at
[lathes.co.uk](https://www.lathes.co.uk/smartbrown), put in my own words, and
none of it has yet been checked against this machine. Sizes are centre height
by distance between centres.

| Model | Made | Size | What to look for | Spindle bearings |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Model L](https://www.lathes.co.uk/smart-and-brown-model-L) | Early 1940s to the 1990s | About 4 in × 13½ in | A plain-turning lathe after a pre-war Boley: no leadscrew and no screwcutting. Usually a collet closer, often a lever-feed slide, a cut-off slide or a capstan in place of a tailstock. Many variants. | Depends on the headstock: lapped bronze on some, ball and roller races on others. |
| [Model M](https://www.lathes.co.uk/smart-and-brown-model-M) | 1946 to about 1959 | 4 in × 18 in | Also after a Boley. Changewheels on a swinging bracket, with the leadscrew running down the middle of the bed underneath, not along the front. Back gear, and a three-step flat-belt pulley. | [Model A](https://lathes.co.uk/smartbrown/page2.html) | Mark 1 about 1947 to 1957; Mark 2 from 1958 | 4½ in × 18 in | A cast-iron cabinet stand with the speed-change gearbox in its base, worked by a quadrant lever on the front of the stand, and a flat belt up to the headstock. | [Sabel, SAB and S](https://www.lathes.co.uk/sabel) | Late 1940s to 1961 | 4¾ in × 18 in | A copy of the 9-inch South Bend, so it looks like a Boxford. The Sabel has a screwcutting gearbox and a power-feed apron; the SAB has changewheels and power feed; the S has changewheels and hand feed only. The first version changes speed by lever, twelve speeds from 30 to 2,500 r.p.m.; the VSL has push-button variable speed. | Double-row roller, with oil pumped and filtered. |

## The quickest tests

- **The spindle nose.** A plain flange with a ring of cam sockets is a 1024.
  A thread means it is not.
- **The leadscrew.** None at all suggests a Model L. Under the middle of the
  bed points to a Model M, the only one described that way.
- **The stand.** A cast-iron cabinet with a speed lever on its front suggests
  a Model A, though some Model Ms had a gearbox in the base as well. Sheet
  metal with V-belts inside is a Sabel.
- **The centre height.** 4 in, 4½ in, 4¾ in or 5½ in narrows it at once.
